Why DrainSight Matters

Small drainage problems can have a big impact. We're exploring how technology can help communities recognize potential problems earlier.

Why Are Storm Drains Important?

Storm drains help collect rainwater from roads, sidewalks, and other paved surfaces. They are an important part of a community's stormwater system.

When heavy rain occurs, properly functioning drainage systems help move water away from areas where it could accumulate.

What Happens When Drains Become Blocked?

Leaves, litter, and other debris can accumulate around storm drain openings. When enough debris builds up, it can restrict water entering the drain.

This can contribute to standing water and localized flooding, especially during heavy rain. Flooding can also have other causes, including rainfall intensity and drainage system capacity.

Why Does This Matter in Westport?

Westport, Connecticut, experiences seasonal rainfall and storms. Like many communities, it relies on stormwater infrastructure to manage runoff from developed areas.

Recognizing visible debris near storm drains can help raise awareness about drainage maintenance and the importance of keeping public spaces clean.

Our goal: Help make stormwater awareness more accessible through a simple, student-created AI tool.

How Can Artificial Intelligence Help?

Inspecting photographs manually can take time. Image recognition technology can help identify visual patterns in photographs and organize observations into categories.

DrainSight uses a trained image classification model to identify three possible results: Clear, Visible Debris, and Uncertain.

These predictions can help users notice possible concerns, but they do not replace professional inspections.

Real-World Examples

Example 1: A Clear Drain

A photograph shows a storm drain with little visible debris. DrainSight may classify the image as Clear.

Example 2: Leaves Covering a Drain

A storm drain is surrounded by fallen leaves. DrainSight may classify the photograph as Visible Debris, indicating that the area could deserve closer attention.

Example 3: A Blurry Photograph

A photograph is too blurry or unclear to evaluate reliably. DrainSight may classify it as Uncertain.

Our Vision for the Future

DrainSight is currently an early-stage student project. With more training images, testing, and feedback, we hope to improve its ability to recognize visible drainage concerns.

In the future, tools like DrainSight could support community education and help organize observations for further review. Our current website does not automatically send reports to local authorities.
